Dotted rhythm

A rhythm shaped by a dotted note and shorter notes around it. A dot adds half of the note’s original value: a dotted eighth followed by a sixteenth has a written long-to-short ratio of 3:1.

Listen for: Focus on the move from the longer note to the shorter one and imagine how it would differ if the time were divided equally. Listening for the next note’s onset, rather than just the decay of the first, makes the long–short placement easier to follow.

Tuplet subdivisions

A tuplet divides a fixed span of time into a different number of notes from the usual division. For example, a triplet divides into three a span normally divided into two. The reference duration matters.

Listen for: Keep a steady beat and follow how many onsets fit inside it. If another voice uses the usual subdivision, compare the two: the change is in how the same duration is divided, not necessarily in tempo.
